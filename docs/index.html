His books give people back the memory of who they are: the spirit of the Father is in everyone, and a person pays their debt with love, not with sacrifice.

What these books give

At the centre of these books is the human being. Not the lowest rung beneath the angels, as has been taught for centuries, but, in the words of The Devil's Scribe Is Always Right, “the only being to whom all the others turn when they need what they themselves cannot do”. The author wants readers to remember who they are: the spirit of our Father is in them, and they pay their debt not in the sacrifices that Enoch taught people to offer, but in “the only currency for which the chancery has no tariff—love”.

About the author

Albert Lashev is the author of investigative books on early Christianity, the papacy and power in European history. He divides his time between Spain and Russia and writes in Russian; his books are also published in English, German, Spanish, French and Italian.

The author reads his sources word for word, marks every claim as document, reconstruction or tradition, names the weak points of his own theories himself and shows where they can be checked. The first book of each cycle is available free of charge.

Books

The books of each cycle are meant to be read in order.

The Greatest Substitution

The cycle The Greatest Substitution is about the man the world knows as Jesus Christ (in these books, Radomir) and about those who stood beside him: Mary Magdalene, John, Philip and Paul. Among the cycle's conclusions: supersubstantial bread (panem supersubstantialem) in the Lord's Prayer in place of the familiar daily bread; two chapters of the Apocalypse transposed, after which the book answers a different question; and the observation that half of the New Testament was written by a man who never met his teacher. The cycle closes with The Devil's Scribe Is Always Right, an investigation into Enoch, the first scribe, and into the place the human being holds in this order of things.

Born in the Matrix. The Trace

The cycle Born in the Matrix. The Trace is about power and about who recognises it: how the names of popes and kings add up to a system, and what stands behind it. It includes an investigative biography of Guillaume de Nogaret, the lawyer who arrested Pope Boniface VIII in 1303.
